HORCH 8, Typ 350, year 1928 ”Touring and Parade Car”

The winner is:

Horch 8, Type 350,
from 1928, winner at the
“European Concours de Elegance”.


One of the rarest and most exclusive pre-war cars:
a Zwickau-built Horch 8 Type 350 with seven-seat open touring and parade coachwork by Hornig of Meerane.
A unique vehicle, considered the only surviving Horch 350 with this spacious, luxurious seven-seat open touring body.

We restored it faithfully in every detail.

After extensively refurbishing the original chassis, axles, drivetrain and steering, we researched and planned the construction of the wooden body structure in our coachbuilding shop.

Little of the original body could be saved. We first rebuilt the large passenger compartment and all structural connections in fine ash, then accurately made doors, seat runners, additional folding seats and other fittings.

Our body shop then faithfully fabricated all the metal panels to clothe the timber structure.

We also recreated many important fittings: substantial bronze door hinges and windscreen frames, complete locks, handles, trim, headlights and other parts, largely in brass.

We reconstructed the large, mechanically complex folding hood frame too.

The original Horch eight-cylinder engine, unfortunately completely dismantled with many missing parts.

Aluminium straight-eight engine,
4 litres, 80 PS,
twin overhead camshafts with vertical-shaft drive — exceptionally sophisticated engineering for 1928.

A remarkable example of German automotive engineering, then as now. This distinctive engine was designed by Paul Daimler, Horch’s chief designer and son of Gottlieb Daimler, while working for one of Mercedes-Benz’s major competitors.
An extraordinary connection…

Precision line-boring of the crankshaft bearing tunnel in the aluminium block.

Meticulous preparation, including newly made crankshaft, camshaft and connecting-rod bearings and specially manufactured pistons.

Line-boring the new bearing tunnels for both camshafts in the cylinder head.

The two large overhead camshafts with special gearing, complete with
vertical-shaft drive.

Engine-building artistry from 1928.

After painstaking assembly, complex even by modern standards, the engine was prepared for its first test-bench run in decades.
The result: silky-smooth running. Remarkable and unique.
Almost century-old engineering with such refinement inspires respect,
even among our experienced technicians and restorers.

We also extensively overhauled the substantial gearbox, all bearings, nine-plate clutch and attached Dewandre vacuum brake servo.

Some of the countless newly made or refurbished trim pieces and fittings.

The crowned Horch badge and winged arrow — symbols of excellence in automotive design.

What a car!

Even from this angle and in unfinished form, the scale and exclusivity of this Horch luxury car are evident.

With this model range, Horch led Germany’s large eight-cylinder luxury segment. Almost 3,000 Horch 8 Type 350s were built in various body styles.

Our paint specialists also mastered the authentic four-colour finish and coachlines.

The completed eight-cylinder engine, restored with immense effort and expertise: 4 litres, 80 PS.

The crowned Horch badge and winged arrow already expressed the marque’s ambition to build cars of the highest class.


Following extensive paint, electroplating and upholstery work, we completed every mechanical and electrical detail.

Now for a particular historical detail:

an original photograph of this very vehicle in 1928.

The man wearing a bowler hat on the left is Paul von Hindenburg, then President of the Weimar Republic.

Years of detailed research suggest this restored car, with its special body, was associated with President Paul von Hindenburg: a one-off touring and parade car by Hornig of Meerane.

Following an extensive, faithful rebuild, this successful and subsequently award-winning restoration from Die Oldtimermanufaktur could be presented to a wider public.

We were invited to show it among outstanding restorations at the European Concours d’Elegance during ClassicGala at Schwetzingen’s Baroque palace in Baden-Württemberg.

The result: three awards and trophies for this special car and our first-class restoration.

first prize, “Best of Show” for the best-restored car.

first prize, “Best of ClassicGala” / “Star of Classics” as overall winner.

Second place. “Hans Hedtke Prize”.

Our entire team is proud that an international jury of more than thirty experts recognised the quality and outstanding result of our work, helping preserve this unique witness to German automotive history.
