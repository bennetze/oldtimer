Hanomag Sportroadster, year 1936

Return of a “world star”.

(Press quotation.)

This superbly restored Hanomag Sport Roadster is no ordinary pre-war sports car: it won numerous victories, particularly in the 1930s.

It is regarded as the original winner of Morocco’s 1937 International Desert Rally, driven by the legendary man later celebrated as the “racing baron”,
Huschke von Hanstein,
and is probably the sole survivor of the three rally cars specially built for that purpose.

We carried out a comprehensive, faithful and authentic restoration for its owner at Die Oldtimermanufaktur.

Discover the car’s remarkable history and racing success with Huschke von Hanstein, including victory in Morocco’s International Desert Rally in May 1937.

Painstaking archive research, including our own historical collection assembled over many years, uncovered fascinating details about this celebrated Sport Roadster.

It too had suffered questionable, often amateur repairs by previous owners over the decades.

Large areas of the body could no longer simply be repaired and had to be entirely remade.

This included the extensive load-bearing timber framework and the complete aluminium outer skin.

Damaged frame sections were newly fabricated and replaced, and the entire floor renewed.

The completed, faithfully recreated structural timber framework with integrated spare-wheel well, as aluminium panelling begins.

Once the timber structure was renewed, the entire outer body skin had to be fabricated section by section in special aluminium sheet.

The solid brass radiator shell was also painstakingly restored and partly renewed.

The nearly complete rebuilt body, with all fittings being aligned.

Our workshop had expanded to include its own fully equipped paint shop, allowing this car to receive a faithful, high-quality finish incorporating the client’s wishes.

The painted body, once again in original cream white with blue details and the distinctive original HANOMAG lettering incorporated into the finish.

The wire wheels were irreparably worn and usable only as patterns. Alongside painting, we remade all five: new rims, drilling and dimpling, painting rims and centres to specification, making and chroming all spokes, and lacing seventy spokes into each wheel.

The engine initially seemed an almost insoluble problem: dismantling revealed frost cracks, severe stress cracks and disastrous corrosion.

As it was effectively beyond viable repair, a long search found a better original replacement block, which still required extensive welding and entirely new internals.

Carburettor, ancillaries and instruments were faithfully refurbished, and a new stainless-steel fuel tank was made.

Alongside bodywork and paint, our mechanical department repaired the complete mechanical systems and painstakingly remade axle components.

Our finishing department then assembled all refurbished components, while we made an entirely new wiring loom and renewed the complete interior in our upholstery shop.

The result is unique: of the three cars originally built, this restoration has preserved at least one as a mechanically and historically valuable piece of cultural heritage for future generations.

Following completion and extensive testing, the car made its first major appearance on 3 October 2014 at the former racing course near Dessau, Saxony-Anhalt.

Background: in 1939, several world records were set on a specially prepared high-speed section of the newly opened Berlin–Leipzig autobahn, today’s A9, near Dessau. In the months before the Second World War, leading German and international manufacturers used this track for testing and record attempts.

One record car was a Hanomag diesel with a specially hand-built streamlined body, achieving previously unexpected top speeds. At the time, Hanomag AG in Hanover-Linden was a major, successful German vehicle manufacturer with its own engine production.

Seventy-five years later, the Hanomag interest group and Hugo Junkers Museum arranged a complete closure of this surviving A9 section beneath Dessau’s legendary Bauhaus bridges on German Unity Day, 3 October 2014. Numerous classic-car enthusiasts and thousands of spectators revived the spirit of that spectacular racing era.

The “Rekordwoche Dessau” took place on the historic grounds of the Junkers aircraft works in the city associated with Bauhaus and Junkers, centred on the Hugo Junkers Museum.

Alongside the 1939 Hanomag diesel world-record car, our restored Hanomag Sport Roadster took part with its proud owner.

A unique occasion: after almost eighty years, on historic ground at the Hugo Junkers technology museum, beneath the legendary
“Tante Ju”, the Junkers Ju 52.

After a challenging but highly successful restoration, we ceremonially returned this historic Sport Roadster to its proud owners.

May it continue to take part successfully in historic events…
