OPEL Admiral "Hebmüller", year 1938

OPEL ADMIRAL “Hebmüller”
Sport Cabriolet
from 1938.

“DER GOLDENE KLASSIKER”

awarded

“Best Restoration of the Year”

History: the pre-war Opel Admiral is widely regarded as the largest and perhaps most luxurious Opel ever built. During its mere two years of production, this flagship successfully established Opel in the luxury segment, before the outbreak of the Second World War brought this to an abrupt end.

Engineering: the Opel Admiral had a smooth six-cylinder engine displacing 3.6 litres and producing around 80 PS, intended to allow a top speed of approximately 140 km/h.

Body styles: the 1938 Admiral was offered by the factory as a substantial four-door saloon and, in small numbers, an imposing four-door “factory cabriolet”. In fact, the latter had coachwork by the renowned Gläser firm in Dresden. A few special bodies were also supplied by independent coachbuilders including Gläser, Hebmüller, Autenrieth and Petera: Pullman saloons, ambulances and hearses, but above all elegant, sporty two-door cabriolets.

Among these coachbuilders, Hebmüller of Barmen/Wuppertal consistently stood out for its particularly elegant and assured styling.

Its gifted designers gave Hebmüller a remarkable flair for exclusive, beautifully proportioned bespoke bodies built to exceptionally high standards.

A rare original photograph of an Admiral Hebmüller Sport Cabriolet at the 1937 IAMA motor show on Berlin’s Kaiserdamm.

Despite its disastrous as-found condition, traces of the car’s original sophistication remained, allowing a knowledgeable observer to recognise that it had once been something very special.

Photographs taken during the extensive body reconstruction.

The entire interior was also reconstructed from original documentation, and the sporty convertible hood was made with authentic horsehair insulation.

New seat spring units and the completed seats, faithfully recreated to original specification.

The result is a true automotive jewel.

The refurbished instruments and elegant fittings — silver-plated as originally supplied, rather than simply chromed.

The stylishly sculpted flanks, enormous doors mounted on three substantial hinges each, two-tone paintwork and numerous Art Deco features reveal a wealth of striking, beautifully shaped details. Together, they make this Admiral Hebmüller Sport Cabriolet an exceptionally elegant and tasteful design.

Only about 15 examples of this early Sport Cabriolet body are said to have been built by Hebmüller in 1938, featuring rear-hinged doors, individually opening windscreens and semaphore indicators integrated into the screen frames. Today, only one other car, in poor condition in Lithuania, is known alongside this now superbly restored example.

The unique character of this beautiful car justified the immense restoration effort.

Beautiful, almost playful Art Deco details. The original coachbuilder’s badge of Hebmüller, Wuppertal/Barmen.

The result surprised more than just us: this magnificent car subsequently received the official “Der Goldene Klassiker” award and was named “Best Restoration of 2024”.


Presented by a distinguished jury and Auto Bild Klassik magazine, the award was handed to us at the official ceremony in November 2024 at the Mercedes-Benz Museum in Stuttgart.

The “Goldener Klassiker” award for “Restoration of the Year 2024”.

​

We are proud that this high-quality, faithful restoration preserves such a precious piece of automotive history for future generations. We thank the organisers for this wonderful award for an equally remarkable car.
